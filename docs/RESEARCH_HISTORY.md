# Curated Mathematical Development

The problem combines the ordinary necklace lower bound with a literal
reverse-complement constraint. The odd obstruction is local and robust: a
primitive rotation cycle links two fixed lower states, so one deletion cannot
destroy both RC-opposed routes.

The even mechanism is global. The binary cyclotomic construction first
reveals that the seed's RC defect is an exact gradient. The decisive
alphabet-general observation is to replace raw symbols by injective
antisymmetric real weights. Every covariance, ray, zero-sector, and
source-firing argument then survives over an arbitrary fixed-point-free
complemented alphabet. Injectivity is precisely what turns a zero-to-zero
residual step into a pure rotation.

The same weighted geometry yields more than existence. On positive lower
states, the real part of `P-W` is strictly increasing along every residual
edge and changes sign under lower reverse complement. Its negative half,
with one lexicographic choice from every zero-level pair, is a self-dual order
ideal. Firing that ideal gives a deterministic direct terminal. Cyclotomic
polynomial reduction and an algebraic norm gap then turn the integer-weight
specialization into an exact polynomial-bit membership theorem.

The retained executable panel has a deliberately narrower purpose. It checks
the binary specialization through `k=12`, with two independent exact
implementations and mutation controls. It is preserved because it exercises
the delicate ray, gradient, zero-tie, and source-move conventions; it is not
presented as evidence for an alphabet-general quantifier.
