\section{The cyclotomic seed and zero-sector ties}
\label{sec:seed}

For the remainder of the proof, let $k\geq4$ be even and specialize to the
binary alphabet.  Put $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/k}$.  For
$w=x_0\cdots x_{k-1}$ define
\[
  P(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}x_j\zeta^{j+1},
  \qquad
  W(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}x_j.
\]
On a lower word $u=u_0\cdots u_{k-2}$ define
\[
  \lambda(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}u_j
    \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi(j+1)}k\right),
  \qquad
  \chi(u)=\ind[\lambda(u)>0].
\]

\begin{lemma}[Covariance]
\label{lem:covariance}
For every length-$k$ word $w$,
\[
  P(Rw)=\zeta^{-1}P(w),\qquad
  P(\RC w)=-\overline{P(Rw)},\qquad
  P(R\RC w)=-\overline{P(w)}.
\]
Moreover
\[
  \operatorname{Im}P(w)=\lambda(t(w)),\qquad
  \operatorname{Im}P(Rw)=\lambda(h(w)),
\]
and $\lambda(\RC u)=\lambda(u)$ on lower words.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The rotation identity follows by reindexing and using $\zeta^k=1$.
For reverse complement, the sum of all $k$th roots of unity is zero, so
\[
  P(\RC w)
  =\sum_i(1-x_{k-1-i})\zeta^{i+1}
  =-\sum_jx_j\zeta^{-j}
  =-\overline{P(Rw)}.
\]
Rotating once gives the third identity.  The omitted last bit of $t(w)$
would multiply $\zeta^k=1$ and contributes no imaginary part; the head
identity follows by applying the same observation to $Rw$.  Finally,
reindexing the lower sum and using
$\sin(2\pi(k-r)/k)=-\sin(2\pi r/k)$ proves lower-word invariance.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The selector}

On each PCR with $P\neq0$, select one word by the following rule:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item select the point on the negative real axis, if it occurs;
  \item otherwise select the unique $w$ such that
  $\operatorname{Im}P(w)<0<\operatorname{Im}P(Rw)$.
\end{enumerate}
On a PCR where $P$ vanishes identically, select an arbitrary representative
for now.  Denote the resulting one-per-PCR set by $M$.

\begin{lemma}[Full period and uniqueness]
\label{lem:selector-unique}
Every nonzero embedded PCR has full period $k$, and the selector above is
unique.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If a word in a nonzero PCR had period $d<k$, then
$P(R^dw)=\zeta^{-d}P(w)=P(w)$ would force $P(w)=0$.  The nonzero rotations
therefore form the $k$ vertices of a regular polygon.  Since $k$ is even,
real vertices occur in antipodal pairs.  If the negative ray is absent,
there is no real vertex at all, and clockwise rotation gives exactly one
strict crossing from the lower to the upper half-plane.  Otherwise the
negative-ray vertex is unique.
\end{proof}

The zero sector must be handled explicitly: choosing its representatives
carelessly could appear to affect cycles.  The next theorem shows that it
does not.

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary-zero-tie acyclicity]
\label{thm:arbitrary-zero}
For every choice of representatives on zero-embedding PCRs, the set $M$ is
an ordinary MDS.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define $Q(w)=P(w)-W(w)$.  If
$y=x_1\cdots x_{k-1}a$ follows $w=x_0\cdots x_{k-1}$ and
$\delta=a-x_0$, then
\[
  P(y)=\zeta^{-1}P(w)+\delta,
  \quad W(y)=W(w)+\delta,
  \quad Q(y)=-W(w)+\zeta^{-1}P(w).
\]
Thus $Q(y)$ is obtained by rotating $Q(w)$ clockwise by $2\pi/k$ about the
real center $-W(w)$.

A residual path cannot move from $\operatorname{Im}P\leq0$ to
$\operatorname{Im}P>0$: a strict lower-to-upper crossing is selected, and a
departure from the negative ray is selected.  From the positive ray the
clockwise rotation enters the lower half-plane; from the origin the next
imaginary coordinate is zero.  A residual directed cycle must therefore lie
entirely in the open upper half-plane or in the closed lower half-plane.

Write $\theta=2\pi/k$ and $P(w)=\rho e^{i\phi}\neq0$.  Along a residual
edge within the upper region,
\[
  \operatorname{Re}Q(y)-\operatorname{Re}Q(w)
  =2\rho\sin(\theta/2)\sin(\phi-\theta/2)>0.
\]
Along a residual edge within the closed lower region the same quantity is
strictly negative: the negative-ray starting point was selected, and the
remaining angular interval is $\pi+\theta\leq\phi\leq2\pi$.  Hence neither
region contains a residual cycle unless every edge of that cycle begins at
$P(w)=0$.

If consecutive labels $w$ and $y$ both have zero embedding, then
$P(y)=a-x_0$ forces $a=x_0$.  Every step is a pure rotation.  Such a cycle
is exactly a zero-embedding PCR, from which one word was selected.  This is
again impossible in the residual.  Therefore the residual lower graph is a
DAG.  Since $M$ selects one word from every PCR, it attains the lower bound
$N_2(k)$ and is an ordinary MDS.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Equivariant zero ties}

Lemma~\ref{lem:covariance} shows that reverse complement permutes the
zero-embedding PCRs.  On a paired orbit $\{C,\RC(C)\}$, choose $w\in C$ and
$\RC(w)$ in the mate.  On an RC-stable PCR, choose either of its two
RC-fixed rotations; Appendix~\ref{app:technical} proves that exactly two
exist.  These choices make the zero-sector part of $M$ invariant without
altering the MDS property from Theorem~\ref{thm:arbitrary-zero}.
