\section{The odd-order obstruction}
\label{sec:odd}

\begin{theorem}[Odd obstruction]
\label{thm:odd}
For every odd $k=2m+1\geq3$, no reverse-complement-invariant decycling set
in $B(q,k)$ has cardinality $N_q(k)$.  Its cardinality exceeds $N_q(k)$ by
at least two.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a complementary pair $a,\bar a$ and define the length-$2m$ lower states
\[
  f_m=(a\bar a)^m,\qquad g_m=(\bar a a)^m.
\]
Both are fixed by reverse complement.  The length-$k$ word
\[
  w_m=(a\bar a)^m\bar a
\]
contains $m$ copies of $a$ and $m+1$ copies of $\bar a$.  If it were a
proper $r$-fold power, then $r>1$ would divide both counts, contradicting
$\gcd(m,m+1)=1$.  Its PCR therefore has full length $k$.

In the lower-state graph this PCR contains the direct arc $g_m\to f_m$ and,
through its other $k-1$ arcs, a directed path from $f_m$ to $g_m$.  A
decycling set of cardinality $N_q(k)$ selects exactly one arc of this PCR.
Deleting the direct arc leaves the long path; deleting any other arc leaves
the direct path.  Thus one endpoint-to-endpoint direction survives.

If the deletion set were reverse-complement invariant, the surviving path
would have an image directed from the other fixed endpoint back to the first.
Their concatenation is a nonempty closed directed walk and contains a
directed cycle, a contradiction.

It remains to justify the stated gap.  At odd length no word is
reverse-complement fixed, so every invariant set has even cardinality.
Reverse complement also acts freely on the PCRs.  Indeed, suppose a PCR of
odd minimal period $d$ were setwise stable, so that
$\RC(w)=R^s w$ for some $s\in\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z$.  On rotation indices
reverse complement induces $r\mapsto s-r$.  Every reflection of an odd
cycle has a fixed index, which would force the corresponding letter to equal
its complement.  Therefore the PCRs occur in free pairs and $N_q(k)$ is
even.  Strict excess over $N_q(k)$ is consequently at least two.
\end{proof}

The proof does not determine the exact odd excess; no formula inferred from
small odd orders is used below.
