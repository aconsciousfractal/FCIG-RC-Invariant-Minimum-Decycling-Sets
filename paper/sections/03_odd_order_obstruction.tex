\section{The odd-order obstruction}
\label{sec:odd}

The negative direction is not specific to the binary alphabet.

\begin{theorem}[Odd obstruction]
\label{thm:odd}
Let $\A$ be a finite alphabet with a fixed-point-free complement.  For every
odd $k=2m+1\geq3$, no reverse-complement-invariant decycling set in
$B(q,k)$ has cardinality $N_q(k)$.  Its cardinality exceeds $N_q(k)$ by at
least two.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a complementary pair $a,\bar a$ and define two length-$2m$ lower states
\[
  f_m=(a\bar a)^m,
  \qquad
  g_m=(\bar a a)^m.
\]
Both are fixed by reverse complement.  Consider the length-$k$ word
\[
  w_m=(a\bar a)^m\bar a.
\]
It contains $m$ copies of $a$ and $m+1$ copies of $\bar a$.  If it were a
proper $r$-fold power, then $r>1$ would divide both counts, contradicting
$\gcd(m,m+1)=1$.  Thus its PCR has full length $k$.

In the lower-state graph this PCR contains the direct arc $g_m\to f_m$ and,
using all its other $k-1$ arcs, a directed path from $f_m$ to $g_m$.
Any decycling set of cardinality $N_q(k)$ selects exactly one arc of this
PCR.  Deleting the direct arc leaves the long path; deleting any other arc
leaves the direct path.  Hence one of the two endpoint-to-endpoint
directions remains.

Suppose the deletion set is reverse-complement invariant.  Since both
endpoints are fixed and reverse complement reverses arrows, the surviving
path has a reverse-complement image directed from the other endpoint back to
the first.  Their concatenation is a nonempty closed directed walk, which
contains a directed cycle.  This contradicts decycling.

At odd length no word is reverse-complement fixed, so every invariant set has
even cardinality.  Reverse complement also acts freely on PCRs.  Indeed, if
an odd-period PCR were setwise stable, then for some word $w$ of odd minimal
period $d$ and some $s\in\Z/d\Z$ we would have $\RC(w)=R^s w$.  On rotation
indices reverse complement induces the reflection
$r\mapsto s-r$ modulo $d$.  (The closely related position-index formula is
$i\mapsto s-1-i$.)  Every reflection of an odd cycle has a fixed
index, which would force the corresponding letter of $w$ to equal its own
complement, contrary to the fixed-point-free hypothesis.  Thus PCRs occur in
free reverse-complement pairs, so $N_q(k)$ is even.  Strict excess is
therefore at least two.
\end{proof}

The proof does not determine the exact odd excess.  In particular, no
formula inferred from small odd orders is needed anywhere in this paper.
