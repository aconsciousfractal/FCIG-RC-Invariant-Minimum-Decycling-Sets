\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Minimum decycling sets in directed de Bruijn graphs are both classical
combinatorial objects and a source of finite-window sampling constructions.
The ordinary minimum is governed by rotation cycles and was established by
Mykkeltveit's construction and later unavoidable-set formulations
\cite{Mykkeltveit1972,ChamparnaudHanselPerrin2004}.  Modern work has made the
move structure of minimum decycling sets explicit
\cite{MarcaisDeBlasioKingsford2024}.  Reverse-complement symmetry introduces
a different constraint: a chosen word must be accompanied by the reverse
complement of that word.  This constraint is natural in canonicalized
sequence processing and was stated directly in the k-nonical setting
\cite{MarcaisElderKingsford2024}.

This paper settles the binary existence question.

\begin{theorem}[Binary parity theorem]
\label{thm:parity}
For every binary word length $k\geq2$, the directed de Bruijn graph
$B(2,k)$ has a reverse-complement-invariant ordinary minimum decycling set
if and only if $k$ is even.
\end{theorem}

The two directions have different mechanisms.  At odd order, a primitive
pure-rotation cycle has two reverse-complement-fixed lower endpoints.  One
deletion from that cycle cannot destroy both endpoint-to-endpoint directions,
and reverse complement supplies the opposing residual path.  At even order,
we exploit a feature of the classical cyclotomic construction which is not
visible from a generic symmetry average.  After resolving the zero-embedding
ties equivariantly, the reverse-complement defect of the Mykkeltveit set is
the exact gradient of an open-half-plane indicator.  Legal source firings
then remove the defect monotonically.

The proof is constructive in the following precise sense.  It gives an
initial ordinary minimum set, an integral nonnegative defect potential, and
a finite legal firing rule.  It does not claim a new complexity bound or an
optimal firing length.

\paragraph{Relation to prior terminology.}
The phrase ``symmetric decycling set'' has also been used for the ordinary
Mykkeltveit construction based on opposite geometric half-planes
\cite{PellowEtAl2023}.  We therefore use the unambiguous phrase
\emph{reverse-complement-invariant} throughout.

\paragraph{Evidence discipline.}
The all-order proof occupies Sections~\ref{sec:odd}--\ref{sec:parity-proof}.
Section~\ref{sec:validation} records an independent exact finite
implementation only as a sign, convention, and mutation check.  No finite
census is a premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}.

\paragraph{Organization.}
Section~\ref{sec:normalization} freezes the graph, rotation, and gradient
conventions.  Section~\ref{sec:odd} proves the odd obstruction.  Sections
\ref{sec:seed}--\ref{sec:even} give the even construction.  Section
\ref{sec:parity-proof} composes the two halves and records exact scope.
The appendices collect convention maps, technical details, and the finite
assurance table.

\begin{boundary}
The theorem is binary on the even side.  The odd obstruction holds for every
finite alphabet with a fixed-point-free complement, but no alphabet-general
even theorem, exact odd excess, biological performance statement, or
priority claim is made here.
\end{boundary}
