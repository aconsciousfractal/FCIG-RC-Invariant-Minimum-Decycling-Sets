\section{Legal defect descent and even existence}
\label{sec:even}

We now convert the exact gradient defect into a finite sequence of valid
$F$-moves.  Reverse complement carries an ordinary MDS to an ordinary MDS,
because it reverses directed cycles bijectively.

\begin{lemma}[Positive-potential firing]
\label{lem:positive-firing}
Let $A$ and $N$ be ordinary MDSs and suppose
\[
  \ind_N-\ind_A=\nabla q
\]
for an integral lower-state potential $q\geq0$ with minimum zero.  If
$A\neq N$, some lower state $u$ with $q(u)>0$ admits a valid $F$-move from
$A$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assume otherwise.  At every positive state $u$, failure of the source
condition supplies an incoming nonloop residual arc $v\to u$; a residual
loop is already impossible because $A$ is decycling.  Since that arc is not
in $A$,
\[
  \ind_N(v\to u)=q(v)-q(u)\in\{0,1\},
\]
so $q(v)\geq q(u)>0$.  Repeating the predecessor choice in the finite set of
positive states eventually creates a directed cycle entirely outside $A$,
contradicting that $A$ is a decycling set.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Defect-paired update]
\label{lem:paired-update}
Suppose the current ordinary MDS $A$ satisfies
\[
  \ind_{\RC(A)}-\ind_A=\nabla q,
  \qquad q\geq0,
  \qquad q\circ\RC=q,
\]
with minimum zero.  If $A$ is not RC invariant, let $u$ be an enabled
positive state from Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-firing}, and fire $u$.  The new
ordinary MDS $A'$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \ind_{\RC(A')}-\ind_{A'}
   =\nabla\bigl(q-\ind_u-\ind_{\RC(u)}\bigr).
  \label{eq:paired-update}
\end{equation}
The new potential is nonnegative, RC invariant, has minimum zero, and its
coordinate sum is two smaller.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A valid firing changes $\ind_A$ by $\nabla\ind_u$ and preserves ordinary-MDS
status.  Since reverse complement reverses lower arcs,
\[
  \RC(\nabla\ind_u)=-\nabla\ind_{\RC(u)}.
\]
Subtracting the update of $A$ from the update of its RC image gives
\eqref{eq:paired-update}.  Positivity is paired because $q\circ\RC=q$.
The lower words have odd length $k-1$, so none is RC fixed; $u$ and
$\RC(u)$ are distinct positive states.  Subtracting one at those two
coordinates preserves nonnegativity and invariance.  A previously zero
coordinate is untouched, so the minimum remains zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Even existence]
\label{thm:even}
For every even $k\geq4$, $B(2,k)$ has an RC-invariant ordinary MDS.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Start from the tie-symmetrized ordinary MDS $M$ constructed in
Section~\ref{sec:seed}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:coboundary} supplies the initial
potential $q=\chi$, which is binary, nonnegative, RC invariant, and has
minimum zero.

If the current set is not RC invariant, apply
Lemmas~\ref{lem:positive-firing} and~\ref{lem:paired-update}.  Every step
preserves ordinary-MDS status and lowers the nonnegative integer
$\sum_uq(u)$ by two.  Hence the process terminates.  At termination the set
must be RC invariant: otherwise another positive firing exists.  Equivalently,
if the terminal defect were zero while $q$ were nonzero, then $\nabla q=0$;
connectedness of the lower binary de Bruijn graph would make $q$ constant,
and its retained minimum zero would force $q=0$.  Thus the terminal ordinary
MDS is literally fixed by reverse complement.
\end{proof}

Only the selected state $u$ is fired at each step.  The word ``paired'' in
Lemma~\ref{lem:paired-update} refers to the forced two-coordinate update of
the RC defect potential, not to an unproved simultaneous-move operation.

For clarity, the constructive content of the proof can be read as the
following exact procedure.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Construct the Mykkeltveit seed and resolve its zero PCRs by the
  RC-equivariant tie rule of Section~\ref{sec:seed}.
  \item Compute the binary potential $q=\chi$ from the open half-plane.
  \item While $q\neq0$, choose any positive lower state enabled by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-firing}, fire that single state, and replace
  $q$ by $q-\ind_u-\ind_{\RC(u)}$.
  \item Return the terminal selected set.
\end{enumerate}
\par\smallskip\noindent
The proof establishes existence of a choice at every iteration and exact
termination.  This description is not a claim of an optimized implementation
or of a particular complexity bound.
