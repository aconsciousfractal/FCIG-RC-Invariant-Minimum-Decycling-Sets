\section{Normalization and prior work}
\label{sec:normalization}

\subsection{Words, graphs, and reverse complement}

Let $\A$ be a finite nonempty alphabet of cardinality $q$, equipped with a
fixed-point-free involution $a\mapsto\bar a$.  Thus $q$ is even.  The binary
specialization has $\bar0=1$, and the DNA specialization has
$\bar A=T$, $\bar C=G$.  For $w=w_0\cdots w_{k-1}$ define
\[
  \RC(w)_i=\overline{w_{k-1-i}}.
\]
The directed vertex de Bruijn graph $B(q,k)$ has vertex set $\A^k$ and an
arc $u\to v$ exactly when
$u_1\cdots u_{k-1}=v_0\cdots v_{k-2}$.  Loops are retained.  Reverse
complement is a directed anti-automorphism:
\[
  u\to v \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \RC(v)\to\RC(u).
\]

A set $M\subseteq\A^k$ is a \emph{decycling set} when
$B(q,k)\setminus M$ is a directed acyclic graph (DAG), and is an
\emph{ordinary minimum decycling set} when its cardinality is minimum among
all decycling sets.  We abbreviate the latter to MDS.  Reverse-complement
invariance means $\RC(M)=M$ as a literal set of labelled words.

The line-graph identity $B(q,k)=L(B(q,k-1))$ lets us regard each length-$k$
word as the labelled lower-state arc
\[
  \tail(w)=w_0\cdots w_{k-2}\longrightarrow
  \head(w)=w_1\cdots w_{k-1}.
\]
Deleting $M$ as vertices of $B(q,k)$ is equivalent to deleting those labelled
arcs from $B(q,k-1)$; acyclicity is equivalent in the two representations.

\subsection{Pure cycling registers and the ordinary minimum}

Let $R(w_0\cdots w_{k-1})=w_1\cdots w_{k-1}w_0$.  A pure cycling register
(PCR) is an orbit under $R$, using distinct rotations only.  Its cardinality
is the minimal period of its words.  PCRs partition $\A^k$, and each is a
directed cycle.  Their number is
\begin{equation}
  N_q(k)=\frac1k\sum_{d\mid k}\varphi(d)q^{k/d}.
  \label{eq:necklace}
\end{equation}
Every decycling set meets every PCR.  The matching construction for arbitrary
finite alphabets is the unavoidable-set theorem of
Champarnaud--Hansel--Perrin \cite{ChamparnaudHanselPerrin2004}; the original
binary construction is due to Mykkeltveit \cite{Mykkeltveit1972}.  Hence the
ordinary minimum cardinality is $N_q(k)$, and an MDS selects exactly one
distinct word from each PCR.  The converse is false: one-per-PCR is only a
local cardinality condition and can leave cycles using several PCRs.  Every
homopolymer $a^k$ is a singleton PCR loop and is a mandatory selection.

\subsection{Full-alphabet source firings}

For $u\in\A^{k-1}$ write
\[
  L(u)=\{au:a\in\A\},\qquad R(u)=\{ua:a\in\A\}.
\]
A forward firing, or full-alphabet $F$-move, is valid at $u$ for a selected
set $M$ when $L(u)\subseteq M$, and then
\[
  F_u(M)=\bigl(M\setminus L(u)\bigr)\cup R(u).
\]
These are literal sets.  The two companion sets overlap only at a
homopolymer loop, which is removed and reinserted.  A valid $F$-move
preserves ordinary-MDS status; this is the standard move of
\cite[Section~4.1]{MarcaisDeBlasioKingsford2024}.  Indeed, before the move
the residual lower graph has no arc entering $u$, whereas afterward it has
no arc leaving $u$.  Any newly created directed cycle would have to enter
$u$ along a restored arc and then leave through a deleted one, which is
impossible.  The result is therefore decycling.  If a newly inserted
outgoing companion other than the common loop had already belonged to
$M$, the result would be a smaller decycling set, contradicting ordinary
minimality; hence cardinality is preserved.

For an integer function $f$ on lower states, fix the gradient convention
\begin{equation}
  (\nabla f)(w)=f(\tail(w))-f(\head(w)).
  \label{eq:gradient}
\end{equation}
The indicator update of a valid firing is $\nabla\ind_u$, including at a
homopolymer loop where both sides vanish.

\subsection{Scope of the imported results}

We import the ordinary cardinality theorem and the standard $F$-move
terminology.  We prove move preservation above, as well as the odd
obstruction, the weighted arbitrary-zero-tie acyclicity
theorem, equivariant zero ties, the exact coboundary, the terminating descent,
and the direct midpoint selector.  The k-nonical paper supplies a dated
formulation of the reverse-complement-constrained problem
\cite{MarcaisElderKingsford2024}; it does not supply the parity theorem or
the construction below.  Our graph-theoretic result does not replace that
paper's optimization, implementation, or window-guarantee analysis.
