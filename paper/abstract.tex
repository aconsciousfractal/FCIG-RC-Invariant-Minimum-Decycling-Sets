\begin{abstract}
Let $B(2,k)$ be the directed binary de Bruijn graph on length-$k$ words,
and let reverse complement act by reversing a word and complementing every
bit.  A minimum decycling set is a smallest vertex set whose deletion makes
the graph acyclic.  We prove that a reverse-complement-invariant minimum
decycling set exists if and only if $k$ is even, for every $k\geq 2$.

The odd obstruction is a single primitive rotation cycle connecting two
reverse-complement-fixed lower states in opposite directions.  For even
$k$, we begin with a tie-symmetrized Mykkeltveit selector.  We prove directly
that arbitrary choices on zero-embedding rotation cycles preserve
acyclicity, choose those ties equivariantly, and establish the exact
half-plane identity
\[
  \ind_{\RC(M)}-\ind_M=\nabla\chi .
\]
Here $\chi$ is the open-upper-half-plane indicator on lower words.  A finite
sequence of legal source firings removes this nonnegative symmetric defect
two coordinates at a time while preserving minimum-decycling-set status,
and terminates at a reverse-complement-fixed set.  An independent exact
implementation through $k=12$ is reported only as convention-level
assurance; it is not used in the all-order proof.
\end{abstract}
